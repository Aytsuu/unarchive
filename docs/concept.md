# CONCEPTUALIZING UNARCHIVE

## What is it?

Most of the time, capstone projects are left rotting after spending months of research, development, and finally defend them. What if those projects could be discovered, appreciated, and even continued by other people? The concept of **Unarchive** is that great ideas shouldn't graduate into obscurity. A platform where those projects can be given a second life.

## At Its Core

### Project preservation
Publish a capstone's story, screenshots, research abstract, demo videos, technologies, contributors, and documentation—even when the original application no longer runs.

### Discovery
Explore projects by university, academic year, discipline, technology, or problem being solved. Help students discover ideas beyond their own institution.

### Recognition
Give original creators lasting credit through contributor profiles, institutional affiliations, and shareable project pages.

### Project revival
Allow owners to mark projects as open for collaboration, continuation, or adoption—with their explicit permission.

## The Six Capabilities

**Authentication** — GitHub or Google sign-in.
**Project publishing** — guided upload of project details, media, and links.
**Project discovery** — searchable public gallery with filters.
**Project detail pages** — attractive, shareable presentations of each capstone.
**Creator profiles** — display the students behind each project.
**Moderation and ownership** — verify submissions, respect university intellectual property, and prevent publishing private data or confidential documents.

## An interesting differentiator: Project lifecycle

Instead of treating projects as static portfolio entries, **Unarchive** could communicate whether they have potential for future development.
